I am a mechanical and biomedical engineering student at the **University of Miami**, completing a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering and an M.S. in Biomedical Engineering. I work at the intersection of **medical robotics, soft robotics, and wearable devices**: designing hardware that helps clinicians reach difficult anatomy and helps patients regain function. I am currently applying to Ph.D. programs in medical and soft robotics.

Current work
======
I am a graduate researcher in the **Robotics and Mechatronics Laboratory** at the University of Miami, advised by Prof. Pinhas Ben-Tzvi, where I lead two projects:

* **Assistive hand exoskeleton** for patients with brachial plexus injury, with actuators on the dorsal side of the fingers for flexion and extension, a computer-vision interface that identifies objects and the grasp they need, and force sensing for secure grasping.
* **Robot-assisted platform for endoscopic spine surgery**, including a simulation of the endoscope and tool manipulators to demonstrate the surgical workflow.

I am also a teaching assistant for Mechanical Design I (MAE 341).

Previous research
======
* **Johns Hopkins University (IMERSE and HAMR labs), 2025–2026:** designed a pneumatically actuated soft robotic sphincterotome for bile and pancreatic duct cannulation, reaching a 180° bending angle with omnidirectional motion and cutting targeting time by 58% versus a conventional device in benchtop tests.
* **Rice University (Avila Research Group), summer 2026:** developed a wearable bioimpedance cap for monitoring brain hydration in older adults, together with a multi-layered finite element head model and an anatomically accurate tissue-mimicking head phantom. I also co-authored a paper on how bioimpedance signals behave under mechanical deformation (*ASME Journal of Applied Mechanics*, 2026).
* **University of Miami:** built a hybrid CNC/FDM printer that prints, finishes, and clears parts without human intervention (capstone, with LECS Energy), and synthesized high-entropy alloys by arc melting.
